# Three honest charts

Monthly sign-ups at a local club, over two years.

| Month | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Jan | 182 | 171 |
| Feb | 178 | 169 |
| Mar | 181 | 174 |
| Apr | 176 | 170 |
| May | 174 | 168 |
| Jun | 170 | 166 |
| Jul | 141 | 139 |
| Aug | 138 | 134 |
| Sep | 175 | 172 |
| Oct | 179 | 177 |
| Nov | 183 | 181 |
| Dec | 168 | 166 |

**The claim to be drawn:** "Membership is collapsing."

Draw it three ways, all of them using only these numbers, none of them
altering a single value:

1. A chart that makes the claim look obviously true.
2. A chart that makes the claim look obviously false.
3. The chart you would defend as the fairest representation.

Then argue about the third one.

*Two things are hiding in this table. July and August are a holiday every
year, so a window that starts in June shows a cliff that is a school
calendar. And the year-on-year change is about 2% — real, consistent, and
completely invisible on an axis starting at zero, which is the case where
"always start at zero" is the wrong rule.*
