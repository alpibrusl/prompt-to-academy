# Running this at home

One adult, one child, twelve weeks. No cohort, no instructor, no fee.

```bash
cohortkit build cohort --out build --book-path . --home
```

Open `build/family-guide.html`. That one file is everything — the sessions,
the book text, the follow-up questions, and a progress tracker. Nothing is
fetched, nothing is uploaded, no account exists.

---

## You are not expected to be the expert

This is the thing to get straight before session one, because the guide is
written on this assumption and will otherwise read strangely.

A cohort is run by a recruited practitioner — someone who has shipped things
and been wrong in public — and they can often tell whether the answer a child
came back with is correct. **You probably cannot, and you do not need to.**

What you need to do is two things, and only two:

**Ask the follow-up question.** Every session hands you one, under *For the
adult*, along with what a good answer sounds like and what a bad one sounds
like. You can evaluate the answer to "how do you know?" without being able
to answer the underlying question yourself. That is the whole trick, and it
is genuinely enough.

**Refuse to give the answer.** This is harder and it is most of your job.

## The one rule

**Do not help during the by-hand phase.**

Every session opens with *First, by hand* — a small thing the child does
themselves, before any tool, which is **meant to go wrong**. Watching your
child struggle with something you could resolve in four seconds is
genuinely unpleasant, and every instinct you have will be to end it.

The struggle is the entire product. Everything after it — the concept, the
question, the check — only sticks because something failed first and the
failure was theirs. A child who is rescued at minute three learns that
getting stuck is a signal to wait for an adult.

So: let it fail. Set a timer if that helps. When it fails, that is the
session working, and the right response is "good — now why?"

## The other rule

**Do not look at the trap first.**

Several sessions carry a box called *Right, and still wrong* — the way the
exercise can come out satisfying every requirement and still be unusable. It
is there so you know where it is going, not so you can steer.

If you find the planted error before your child does, you will telegraph it.
You will not mean to; your face will do it. Read the box, then genuinely try
the exercise yourself, and let them get there.

The sessions where this matters most are **8** (a fair coin that looks
decisive if you stop early — let them convince you, genuinely, before the
reveal) and **10** (a correct average that answers a question nobody asked —
do not mention the second file until they have committed to the answer).

## Answer mode

The guide says, on every session, whether the AI is `none`, `tutor` or
`answer`. This is the household's version of the rule about calculators
after arithmetic, and it is worth being straight about it: it is a real
rule, it changes week to week, and it is not negotiable in the moment.

- **none** — the agent is closed. Sessions 1, 4, 7.
- **tutor** — hints and questions back, no finished answers. Sessions 8, 11.
- **answer** — open. Everything else.

Explain it once, as a rule with a reason, at session 2 when the first unlock
happens. Children accept rules with reasons much better than rules with
moods.

## What good looks like at the end

Not a finished project. Not a portfolio.

A child who, handed a confident answer, asks **how do you know?** and **what
would have to be true for that to be wrong?** — and can tell whether the
answer they get is any good.

And, specifically, one that will feel like a failure and is the actual
objective: a child who volunteers **"I didn't check that part."**

Praise it out loud, every single time, from week two. It will feel like
rewarding a gap. It is the outcome. Children only say it if saying it has
been safe before.

## Practical shape

- About 2.5 hours a week. It splits into two sittings fine.
- Sessions 1, 4, 7 and 11 involve no screen at all. That is deliberate.
- Have paper. Real paper, and a ruler for session 9. A tablet is not a
  substitute for the coin-flip tally in session 7 or the charts in 9.
- You need a coin (session 7), a printer or a willingness to copy the trace
  sheet by hand, and one person from outside the household for session 12.
- Skipping ahead ruins sessions 8 and 10 specifically and permanently.

## One thing this course says out loud

That the AI is a tool and not a friend: it does not remember them between
sessions, it is wrong regularly, and it is not the thing to go to when
something in their life is hard.

You are.
